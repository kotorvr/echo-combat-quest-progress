Maps

All four combat maps and the combat lobby wing load on the Quest 3; Ducky's recordings show the Fission, Dyson, Combustion and Surge arenas. Some surfaces, glass and screens still differ from PC, and in Ducky's play test of the newest build several decals and signs looked wrong (dark or garbled). Art fixes for them are in progress. Match flow and round start

A round starts on its own in Practice AI and Find Combat, and in private matches once a team readies up. In the newest reviewed build the doors to the next area in Fission open: this was checked on the headset, and in Ducky's play test the payload led into the next area. In Ducky's first online match (a Steam Frame against a Quest 3, two rounds on Combustion) the capture point, the scoring and the winner were right and the end-of-match scoreboard showed. Many in-match events still don't happen, such as the capture announcements and the capture marker changing colour. A script-by-script check against the PC found 254 gaps in the first 48 scripts; fixes for them are landing build by build. In the lobby's skirmish room Ducky was blocked in the spawn area; a fix has been found.

Guns

All eight guns are rebuilt and fire on the Quest. In the newest reviewed build the guns attach to the hand in combat again, and in the lobby's firing range they attach and fire: a headset test counted 666 shots and 48 hits on the training dummy. The grenades, tac mods and the arc ordnance are in and run cleanly on the Steam Frame. In Ducky's online match the guns, the healing tac mod and the detonator all worked, with no crash. The project's own servers now offer all eight guns: protostar, quark, blazar and starburst next to the four standard ones, thanks to the gun patch from the EchoTools community. In an earlier play test the detonator could also hurt the player who threw it; a fix is planned. Bots don't shoot back yet.

HUD and scoreboard

The match HUD frame shows, but many HUD and menu elements are still missing in play, including the display above the payload; Ducky's play test of the newest build showed this across all maps. The scoreboard, clocks, capture point and payload displays, name tags and end-of-match banners are rebuilt in reviewed builds. In Ducky's online match the team-room percentages, the equipment grid, the name tags and the end-of-match scoreboard worked; the team scores stayed at 0, the kill and capture announcements didn't show, and the in-map screens were cut off or garbled. The newest build adds a fix that keeps objective markers attached after a respawn, brings back the gun's charge-up animation and shows other players' icons at a distance; its parts ran in a headset test, and a play check is next.

Visual effects

The Quest's renderer has no particle system, so the newest build shows simple stand-in effects (muzzle flashes and sparks) in place of the PC's particles. Steam Frame

The same builds run on the Steam Frame, now with the Frame's own best look: a higher refresh rate than the old 72 Hz, edge smoothing (2x anti-aliasing) and foveated rendering. In a 20-minute combat test it averaged 122 to 137 frames per second with no crash, and Ducky played the first online match on it against a Quest 3. Eye-tracked foveated rendering is being tested. It follows the Quest's progress.

Scripts

How the port works, and where each of the game's combat scripts stands.

Echo Combat's game logic (guns, bullets, the match flow, terminals, the orb, the HUD) lives in about 100 small scripts. Each script is a graph of steps (nodes), compiled to native code. Combat's scripts only ever shipped for the PC's x86-64 processors.

The Quest version of Echo VR has the same engine, but none of these combat scripts. Until a script is rebuilt, the Quest runs an empty placeholder in its place, so the map loads but nothing in it works.

Each script is rebuilt for the Quest's ARM64 processor from the PC version: its graph is recovered, every step's code is translated, and it is linked against the code the Quest engine already has. Some steps use node types the Quest engine never shipped scripts for; those are wired straight to the engine.

Every rebuilt script passes automatic checks, then a separate review, then a test on the headset before it goes into a play build. In the table, 'real' counts the steps that do their actual job on the Quest; the rest still do nothing.
